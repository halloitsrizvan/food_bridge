# Winning pitch structure (source guidance)

## Slide structure

1. **Problem and icebreaker.** State the problem and the specific user facing it. Open with a relatable question to get attention.
2. **Evidence.** Back claims with real surveys, reports, news or magazine pieces. Judges must see it is a real-world issue.
3. **Cost.** Quantify the impact: money, time or other resources lost.
4. **Solution (simple and clear).** Show the home screen or core interface. No heavy jargon or buzzwords. Someone with zero technical knowledge must understand it immediately.

(In the 7-slide version, slides 2 and 3 are merged: three sourced stats plus the single number that shows the urgency.)

## Demo strategy

- **Mirror the user journey.** Walk through the app the way the ideal user would, and say why it was built that way.
- **Prioritise the core feature.** Show the main unique selling point first. Skip nice-to-haves. The product tour is ONE slide; it is a map for a live demo, not a feature list to read out.

## Before and after

State the direct impact. Before: the problem took X hours or cost Y. After: a fraction of that. Let judges visualise the value instantly. Only claim what the design supports; label targets as targets.

## Technical details and team (brief)

Keep architecture to a small stack strip unless judges ask. End with a team or thank-you slide. Do not invent team members; if names are not known, leave them out and tell the user to add them.

## Limitations and future plans

- **Limitations:** be transparent about what was not built or scaled in the time available (mocked or seeded data, manual workflows, unverified accounts, edge cases). Each limitation must be true of the current code.
- **Roadmap:** clear, actionable next steps (features, integrations, scalability, how to take it forward after the hackathon). Each step must follow from a real gap.

## Timing

4 to 5 minutes total. 7 slides, about 35 seconds each. The tour slide may take about 50 seconds if demoing live.
